# Problem Statement

**Classification: Portfolio scenario.**

## The Mandate

Leadership has decided the organization needs an AI coding assistant. That's a direction, not a decision — it doesn't say which tool, doesn't account for the tradeoffs between competing priorities, and doesn't give anyone a defensible basis for the choice once it's made.

## The Real Question

Not "which AI tool is coolest?" but:

> Which option provides the best balance of business value, adoption potential, security, governance, integration, and cost?

## Who Has a Stake

- **Engineering** wants a tool that genuinely improves developer productivity and won't be quietly abandoned after initial rollout.
- **Security** wants assurance about how code and data are handled, especially for proprietary or sensitive systems.
- **Risk / Compliance** wants a defensible governance story before thousands of developers get access.
- **Finance** wants predictable, justified cost at scale.
- **Developers themselves** want a tool that doesn't get in their way — a tool nobody uses delivers zero value regardless of how well it scores on paper.

## Why This Is Hard

Every one of those stakeholders would rank the criteria differently if left to choose alone. The job of this framework is to force those competing priorities into one transparent process instead of resolving them through whoever has the most influence in the room.
